## Purpose

Without this volume, users end up managing separate parts of their lives in isolation, one planner at a time. With it, NexaLife becomes a true Life Operating System. The Life Orchestrator & Universal Planning Engine (LOUPE) is the central intelligence layer that coordinates **all** planners within NexaLife, ensuring that plans from different Life Domains work together instead of competing for a user's time, attention, and energy. LOUPE provides a unified understanding of commitments, priorities, availability, and long-term goals.

This volume is a cross-cutting platform capability layer — like `NLB-09` (Automation) or `NLB-14` (Community) — not a single planner, which is why it holds its own top-level slot rather than a sub-series.

---

## Philosophy

People do not live separate lives for education, career, health, finance, family, travel, and hobbies. **They live one life** — the operational restatement of `NLB-00` Article II. The platform should optimize the whole system, not each planner independently.

---

## Universal Life Graph

Every planner contributes structured information to a shared Life Graph: goals, tasks, events, habits, deadlines, projects, skills, relationships, resources, health activities, financial obligations, travel plans. This is not a new data store — it is the Relationship Model already defined in `NLB-07`, viewed as a single graph rather than per-planner tables. Relationships let Nexa understand how actions in one area influence another.

---

## Global Priority Engine

Priorities consider deadlines, user-defined importance, estimated effort, dependencies, available time, calendar commitments, recovery time, and long-term goals. **The system recommends priorities while allowing users to override them** — LOUPE ranks, it never enforces.

---

## Conflict Detection

Detects overlapping events, unrealistic schedules, conflicting deadlines, excessive workload, and resource conflicts. Nexa suggests resolutions but **does not automatically change user plans without permission** — consistent with `NLB-10`'s User Consent requirements.

---

## Cross-Planner Automation

Examples: an exam week may reduce optional fitness goals; a business trip may adjust study sessions; a major financial milestone may update savings goals; a completed certification may advance career objectives. These are LOUPE-scale instances of the Cross-Domain Automation already defined in `NLB-09` — the same trigger/condition/action lifecycle, spanning Life Domains rather than modules within one. **Users review and approve automation rules**, per `NLB-09`'s Approval Flows.

---

## Universal Goals

Goals may span multiple planners. **Worked example**: "Run a Marathon" links Health, Nutrition, Calendar, Finance (equipment budget), Travel (race logistics), and the Habit Tracker, with progress aggregated into one unified view — the same marathon example worked through `NLB-04`'s cross-domain hubs and `NLB-11`'s multi-agent coordination, now given its own aggregation mechanism at the goal level.

---

## Life Balance Dashboard

A holistic overview of health, learning, career, finances, relationships, personal growth, community participation, and rest/recovery. Dashboards help users identify imbalances **without assigning value judgments** — a low score in one domain is information, not a verdict.

---

## Universal Calendar

A single calendar combining study sessions, work meetings, exercise, bills, travel, events, family commitments, competitions, and community activities. Users can filter by planner or view everything together — this is the Calendar Hub already identified in `NLB-04` (PU-01-010), and LOUPE is the mechanism that actually populates it from every domain.

---

## Universal Timeline

A chronological history of significant activities, achievements, and milestones across all planners — a personal record of long-term progress, extending `NLB-01`'s Life Timeline (PU-01-021) into a genuinely cross-domain record rather than a Personal Life-only one.

---

## Decision Support

Nexa helps compare options — choosing between two study plans, balancing overtime with rest, selecting a training schedule before a race, planning around financial constraints. Recommendations explain assumptions and remain user-controlled, per `NLB-11`'s Decision Support section, exercised here across domains rather than within one.

---

## Life Simulations

For selected scenarios, Nexa can model potential outcomes — *"If I study 10 hours per week instead of 6, how does my preparation timeline change?"* or *"If I increase my monthly savings, when might I reach my goal?"* **Simulations are presented as estimates, not guarantees** — the same discipline `NLB-SP-005`'s Readiness Estimation applies to exam preparedness, generalized to any cross-domain projection.

---

## Universal Search

Searches notes, tasks, goals, events, files, AI conversations, projects, contacts where integrated, and resources, supporting keyword, semantic, filtered, and saved search — this is `NLB-08`'s Global Search, described here at the scale LOUPE actually operates at: everything, everywhere, subject to the same permission boundaries.

---

## Context Engine

Nexa may use current context with permission: active planner, time of day, calendar availability, recent activity, device type, and user preferences, to personalize recommendations while respecting privacy — the platform-wide instance of the Memory Model's context scoping defined in `NLB-06`.

---

## Notification Orchestration

Coordinates reminders to avoid overload: grouping related reminders, delaying non-urgent notifications during focus sessions, and respecting quiet hours. Users maintain full control over notification preferences, per `NLB-08`'s Notification Center — LOUPE is what decides *when* a notification fires across every source, not a new notification system.

---

## Life Insights

Weekly balance summary, time allocation trends, goal progress, habit consistency, planner usage, and achievement highlights. Insights encourage reflection rather than comparison — the LOUPE-scale application of `NLB-11`'s Weekly Review, aggregated across every domain instead of one planner.

---

## Universal AI Coordination

Nexa routes requests to specialist AI systems across planners. **Worked example**: *"Help me prepare for a conference next month"* coordinates the Calendar Planner, Travel Planner, Finance Planner, Study Planner, and Health Planner, and Nexa combines their outputs into one coherent plan — the same example already worked through in `NLB-11`, restated here to show it is LOUPE's Life Graph, not a special case, that makes the coordination possible.

---

## Permissions

Users choose which planners may share information — a Health planner can inform scheduling, a Finance planner can contribute budget constraints, a Study planner can remain fully private. Privacy controls are granular and easy to understand, enforced by `NLB-10`'s Permission Engine at the cross-planner sharing level specifically.

---

## Performance

The orchestration layer stays responsive even as users accumulate years of history across multiple planners — described, consistent with `NLB-07`'s approach to scale, as a required property rather than a fixed numeric target.

---

## Extensibility

New planners integrate by registering their data model, declaring supported events, exposing planner capabilities, and participating in shared workflows where authorized. **No planner requires custom orchestration logic if it follows the platform contracts** — the same extensibility discipline `NLB-05` requires of every planner, restated as LOUPE's specific requirement for cross-planner participation.

---

## Design Principle

Every recommendation from Nexa should consider the user's whole life, not just the currently open planner. The objective is to help users make informed decisions across all life domains **while keeping them in control.**
