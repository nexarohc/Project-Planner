## Purpose

Most study platforms offer either individual learning or simple group chats. NexaLife should provide a complete collaborative learning operating system, where students, teachers, researchers, mentors, schools, and organizations work together in structured, goal-oriented environments. The Collaboration, Community & Research Workspace enables learners to study, teach, mentor, conduct research, and complete projects together — supporting both informal study groups and structured educational environments, while respecting privacy and user permissions throughout (`NLB-10`).

---

## Collaboration Philosophy

Collaboration should encourage shared understanding, constructive discussion, peer learning, accountability, knowledge sharing, and ethical academic behaviour. The objective is **meaningful collaboration, not social engagement for its own sake** — the education-specific reading of `NLB-14`'s Community Philosophy.

---

## Collaboration Types

Personal collaboration, friend groups, study circles, classroom workspaces, university courses, research teams, project teams, mentorship groups, and professional learning communities — each an instance of the workspace concept defined in `NLB-10`, scoped to a shared learning purpose.

---

## Study Groups

Each study group includes a dashboard, shared calendar, shared goals, shared notes, shared whiteboards, shared flashcards, a discussion space, a resource library, a task board, an AI workspace, and a progress overview. Permissions are role-based, per the Group Roles below.

---

## Group Roles

| Role | Typical capability |
| --- | --- |
| Owner | Full control |
| Teacher | Assigns and reviews work |
| Mentor | Guides without administrative control |
| Moderator | Maintains discussion quality |
| Student | Participates and contributes |
| Research Lead | Directs a research project |
| Contributor | Adds content |
| Observer | Read-only |
| Guest | Time- or scope-limited access |

This mirrors the Role-Based Access Control model in `NLB-10` — the Study Planner does not invent a parallel permission vocabulary, it specializes the platform one.

---

## Live Collaboration

Real-time note editing, shared whiteboards, collaborative mind maps, live document editing, presence indicators, comments, mentions, and version history. Users can review changes and restore earlier versions where appropriate — built on `NLB-07`'s Real-Time Collaboration and Versioning capabilities.

---

## Research Workspace

Research projects include objectives, a literature review, references, notes, experiments, tasks, deadlines, team members, progress tracking, and AI assistance from Research AI (`NLB-SP-002`). Projects may be personal or collaborative.

---

## Citation Management

Citation libraries, multiple citation styles, reference organization, AI-assisted bibliography formatting, and duplicate detection. **Users remain responsible for verifying citation accuracy** — AI assistance here is a drafting aid, not a source of academic authority.

---

## Literature Review

Research AI assists with summarizing sources, comparing findings, identifying themes, organizing notes, and highlighting potential research gaps. The AI clearly distinguishes its summaries from original source material — conflating the two would violate `NLB-06`'s requirement to separate fact from inference.

---

## Peer Review

Structured workflows for essay review, research review, code review, presentation review, and design critique, with reviewers providing feedback against configurable criteria — the education-specific application of the Reviewer role defined in `NLB-10`.

---

## Group Task Management

Shared tasks, milestones, deadlines, dependencies, assignments, and progress updates, integrating with each member's personal planner where appropriate — built on the Universal Task Engine defined in `NLB-05`.

---

## Collaborative AI

Study groups may use AI to summarize meetings, organize action items, generate study questions, explain concepts, create revision plans, and produce meeting minutes. The AI identifies when content is AI-generated — the group-context instance of the transparency requirement already established for individual AI question generation in `NLB-SP-005`.

---

## Discussion Spaces

Threaded discussions, topic channels, Q&A, announcements, pinned resources, and search, with moderation tools to maintain constructive discussion — the education-scoped application of `NLB-14`'s Safety & Moderation.

---

## Mentorship

Mentors can schedule sessions, review progress, recommend resources, leave feedback, track milestones, and set goals with learners. **Learners control what personal information is shared** — extending `NLB-14`'s Mentorship section with study-specific session and progress tracking.

---

## Classroom Mode

Educators can create classes, assign work, monitor participation, share resources, schedule lessons, review submissions, and provide feedback. Institutional deployments can customize these workflows, per `NLB-10`'s Enterprise Features applied to a school or university organization.

---

## Project Workspaces

Kanban boards, timelines, documents, shared files, AI brainstorming, meeting notes, decision logs, and deliverables — the Study Planner's configuration of `NLB-05`'s Universal Task Engine and Dashboard Engine for team-based academic and research work. Projects can link to courses or research initiatives.

---

## Knowledge Libraries

Communities can build shared libraries of notes, flashcards, mind maps, templates, whiteboards, reading lists, and curated resources, with versioning and attribution preserved — the collaborative counterpart to `NLB-13`'s Knowledge Packs asset type.

---

## Community Challenges

Reading month, math challenge, coding sprint, research week, language immersion, writing challenge — instances of `NLB-14`'s Real-World Challenges, scoped to learning, with optional leaderboards and collaborative goals.

---

## Events

Study sessions, workshops, seminars, hackathons, journal clubs, office hours, and research presentations, integrated with calendars and notifications via `NLB-14`'s Events section.

---

## Communication

Group announcements, direct messages subject to platform policy, mentions, notifications, and shared agendas. Organizations can configure communication policies, per `NLB-10`'s Organization Administration.

---

## Analytics

Participation, task completion, meeting attendance, resource usage, collaborative activity, and project milestones — group insight that avoids exposing unnecessary personal information, consistent with `NLB-13`'s privacy-bounded creator analytics.

---

## Accessibility

Screen readers, keyboard navigation, captions for supported media, high contrast, localization, and responsive layouts.

---

## Security

Role-based permissions, secure sharing, audit logs, data ownership, organization policies, and privacy controls — enforced by the same mechanisms `NLB-10` and `NLB-07` define, not a parallel collaboration-specific security layer. Sensitive educational or research content stays protected under those same guarantees.

---

## Future Extensibility

The architecture should support virtual classrooms, immersive collaboration, laboratory collaboration, external institution integrations, and advanced research workflows, without requiring the core collaboration model to be redesigned.

---

## Design Principle

Collaboration should help people learn together more effectively without sacrificing individual ownership, privacy, or academic integrity. **Technology should strengthen teamwork while keeping learning at the center.**
